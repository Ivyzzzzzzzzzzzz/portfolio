Reimagining the 24 Solar Terms

An interactive website reinterpreting the Chinese 24 Solar Terms through data, culture, and personal reflection. The goal is to reconnect time with the environment and reimagine intangible cultural heritage as a living, contemporary experience.

Message from home

"Today is the Beginning of Winter. Did you eat dumplings?"

Growing up in China, the 24 Solar Terms quietly shaped everyday life through seasonal foods, traditions, and observations of nature. Yet after studying abroad, I realized they had become little more than calendar notifications that I rarely noticed.

This simple message made me question not only my relationship with the Solar Terms, but also my relationship with seasonal change itself.

The Disconnect

A temporal gap emerges as modern calendrical systems drift away from the climate-based logic of the 24 ST.

Before digital clocks and numerical dates, humans read the world through light, wind, shadows, and natural cycles.

Today, our modern calendar shows holidays, deadlines, and meeting schedules in numbers.

Ecology gets flattened and removed, so the transformation in nature stops being lived when it is no longer experienced.

Time Told by Nature, Not Numbers

What’s 24 Solar Terms

The 24 Solar Terms are a traditional East Asian calendrical system. Rather than measuring time only through numbers, it describes time through transformation in the nature: the first rain of spring, the arrival of frost, ect.

Historically, the 24 Solar Terms were used to guide agricultural labor, helping people know when to sow, harvest, prepare, or adapt to environmental change. Over time, they also became embedded in everyday life through food customs, poetry and festivals.

The system functions not only as a way of counting time, but as a way of living with it.

Literature Review

To better understand how cultural knowledge can be translated across contexts, I reviewed research on cultural memory and phenomenology.

Public Memory & Place Hoelscher & Alderman (2004)

Core finding:
Cultural memory becomes accessible when tied to sensory cues and spatial storytelling.

 Key takeaway:
Unfamiliar cultural heritage is better understood through sensory, experiences, not just abstract text.

Phenology & Seasonal Change Forrest & Miller-Rushing (2010)

Core finding:
Phenological events (plant/animal cycles) are sensitive, universal indicators of seasonal change.

 Key takeaway:
Natural seasonal cues act as a cross-cultural "language" to connect users to ecological rhythms.

Key findings:

People connect through sensory experiences.
Weather and environmental changes are more memorable than facts.
Reflection and observation feel meaningful.
Seasonal awareness is universal.

Design Opportunity

How might we help people reconnect with seasonal rhythms through a contemporary digital experience while making the ecological wisdom of the 24 Solar Terms accessible across cultures?

Designing for Three Layers of Connection

To support different ways of connecting with the seasons, I developed a three-layer framework.

Ecological Layer

Helping users notice seasonal changes through environmental cues (weather patterns, shadows, ect.).

Cultural Layer

Providing access to the traditions, rituals, stories, and knowledge associated with each Solar Term.

Personal Layer

Creating opportunities for users to reflect on and relate seasonal change to their own experiences.

Together, these three layers transform the Solar Terms from information to experience.

From Insights to Features

Using the research insights, I translated user needs into a prioritized feature set.

Because users connected most strongly through observation and reflection, features that encouraged exploration and seasonal awareness were prioritized over content-heavy educational experiences.

Testing & Refinement

To validate the usability and experience of the website, I conducted moderated usability testing (3‑task 6 users) focusing on discoverability, interaction intuitiveness, and cross-page consistency.

Landing Page
Users understood the wheel’s cyclical time concept. Some hesitated to find the wheel’s interactive features.

Winter Solstice Detail Page
 Users understood the three solar‑term phases. The solar longitude panel was unclear. Navigation lacked obvious visual cues. Bilingual content was easy to follow.

Year Calendar Page
 Users grasped the grid’s yearly overview. Wheel‑grid visual links were unclear. Some interactions felt disconnected. Users viewed the page as both tool and artwork.
