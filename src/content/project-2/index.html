“Fix Fast,  Enter Respectfully”

A redesign of the Expo at Forest Park resident app focused on making maintenance requests clearer, easier to submit, and easier to track.

Background

In the summer of 2025, a severe tornado struck St. Louis, causing widespread damage across the city. My apartment building, Expo at Forest Park, sat near the center of the storm and was heavily affected. In the months that followed, residents experienced multiple issues including broken windows, leaking roofs, inconsistent water service, and long periods without air conditioning.

Maintenance staff and contractors frequently entered units to inspect and document damage.

Residents relied on the Expo residential app to report issues and track maintenance requests.

The maintenance section of the App felt confusing and difficult to use.

Research

To verify this anxiety is universal, I analyzed 2025 industry data. The results confirmed a systemic drawback:

How might we...
Submit Request

Residents don’t feel guided while filing a request and want to choose a specific time window at submission, so they second-guess category/priority/evidence and worry about unplanned visits.

How might we help residents file with confidence and speed by using plain language, lightweight guidance, and letting them select (and later adjust) a preferred time window as part of the request?

Request History

After submitting, residents don’t feel in the loop—they’re unsure what’s happening or when, which drives worry, repeated checking, and avoidable contacts.

How might we keep residents transparent and in control of progress by clearly communicating where their request stands, what will happen next, and when to expect movement?

Design Decisions
Based on User Testing

Based on usability testing, I identified several friction points in the maintenance request workflow. These insights informed a set of targeted design decisions aimed at improving clarity, reducing cognitive load, and creating a more guided experience.

1
Remove “Priority” Selection

Users consistently selected “high,” even when guidance was available.
The priority field introduced unnecessary decision-making without meaningful differentiation.

Priority is no longer user-defined and is instead determined by the maintenance team, reducing ambiguity and preventing misuse.

2

Clarify Structure and Language of Input Fields

Users struggled with the relationship between issue location, category, and description. Terminology such as “HVAC” and unclear category hierarchies created hesitation.

Reordered the flow to match user logic: start with issue location (room), then narrow down to relevant facilities, along with simplified labels and guided input to reduce guesswork.

3

Introduce a Defined Time Window for Visits

Users expressed uncertainty when submitting requests, particularly around when maintenance would arrive. This lack of clarity led to second-guessing and concern about unexpected visits.

Added a “preferred time window” selection during submission, allowing users to specify availability and feel more in control of the process.

4

Reframe Request History as an Ongoing Status Loop

After submission, users felt disconnected from the process. The existing history view did not clearly communicate status progression or provide reassurance about what happens next.

Added a “preferred time window” selection during submission, allowing users to specify availability and feel more in control of the process.

Redesigned request history as a clear, status-based timeline.

Emphasized quick access to key details within each request.

Improved visibility of updates and maintenance notes
